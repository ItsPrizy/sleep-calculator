Circadian Rhythm: Your Internal Clock and Sleep

Have you ever noticed that you feel more alert at certain times of the day, or sleepy at others—even when you’ve had the same amount of sleep? That’s your circadian rhythm at work. Understanding this internal clock can help you fall asleep faster and wake up more refreshed.

What Is Circadian Rhythm?

Your circadian rhythm is a natural, internal process that regulates the sleep-wake cycle and repeats roughly every 24 hours. It’s influenced by external cues like light and darkness, which is why you feel sleepy at night and awake during the day.

The word “circadian” comes from the Latin circa diem, meaning “about a day.” This biological clock is found in nearly all living organisms, from plants to humans.

How Does Circadian Rhythm Affect Sleep?

Your brain releases melatonin—a sleep-promoting hormone—when it gets dark, signaling to your body that it’s time to rest. In the morning, light suppresses melatonin, helping you wake up. When your circadian rhythm is aligned with your lifestyle, you fall asleep easily and wake up naturally. When it’s disrupted (for example, due to jet lag, shift work, or late-night screen use), you may experience insomnia, daytime fatigue, and mood swings.

Common Circadian Rhythm Disorders

  • Delayed Sleep Phase Disorder: Falling asleep very late and struggling to wake up early (common in teenagers).
  • Advanced Sleep Phase Disorder: Falling asleep too early and waking up too early (common in older adults).
  • Shift Work Disorder: Disrupted sleep due to working non-traditional hours.
  • Jet Lag: Temporary misalignment after crossing time zones.

How to Reset Your Circadian Rhythm

  1. Get morning sunlight: Exposure to natural light within 30 minutes of waking helps regulate your clock.
  2. Limit blue light at night: Reduce screen time 1–2 hours before bed, or use blue light filters.
  3. Stick to a sleep schedule: Try to go to bed and wake up at the same time every day, even on weekends.
  4. Avoid caffeine after 2 PM: Caffeine can delay your circadian rhythm by blocking adenosine, a sleep-promoting chemical.
  5. Exercise regularly: Physical activity can help deepen sleep, but avoid intense workouts too close to bedtime.
