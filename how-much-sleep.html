How Much Sleep Do I Need?

Sleep is essential for good health, focus, and emotional well-being. Yet millions of Americans struggle to get the right amount. The answer to “How much sleep do I need?” depends on your age, lifestyle, and individual body clock.

Recommended Sleep Hours by Age

The National Sleep Foundation provides general guidelines for daily sleep needs. These are ranges that support optimal functioning for most healthy individuals.

Age Group Recommended Sleep
Newborns (0–3 months) 14–17 hours
Infants (4–11 months) 12–15 hours
Toddlers (1–2 years) 11–14 hours
Preschoolers (3–5 years) 10–13 hours
School-age children (6–13 years) 9–11 hours
Teenagers (14–17 years) 8–10 hours
Adults (18–64 years) 7–9 hours
Older Adults (65+) 7–8 hours

Keep in mind that these are averages. Some people feel rested after 7 hours, while others need a full 9 hours. The best way to know your personal number is to pay attention to how you feel during the day.

Signs You’re Not Getting Enough Sleep

  • Feeling drowsy during meetings, driving, or quiet activities
  • Relying on caffeine to get through the afternoon
  • Waking up groggy even after 8 hours in bed
  • Irritability, poor concentration, or memory issues
  • Falling asleep within minutes of lying down (may indicate sleep debt)

Understanding Sleep Cycles

While you sleep, your brain cycles through different stages, including light sleep, deep sleep, and REM (rapid eye movement). A typical cycle lasts about 90 minutes. Waking up in the middle of a deep sleep stage can leave you feeling more tired than before.

That’s why it’s often better to plan your wake-up time based on 90-minute cycles. For example, if you want to wake at 7:00 AM, you might aim to fall asleep at 11:30 PM (5 cycles) or 1:00 AM (4 cycles). Our sleep calculator below can do the math for you.

Final Thoughts on Healthy Sleep

Consistency is more important than perfection. Going to bed and waking up at the same time every day—even on weekends—helps regulate your internal clock and improves sleep quality. Create a relaxing bedtime routine, keep your bedroom cool and dark, and avoid screens 30–60 minutes before sleep.

If you’re still struggling despite making changes, consider talking to a doctor. Sleep disorders like insomnia or sleep apnea are treatable.
